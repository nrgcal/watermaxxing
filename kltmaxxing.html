Official Learning Objective Probably

By the end of this appendix the trainee will understand the final and most dangerous stage of the Know Like and Trust principle.

This stage is called:

KLTmaxxing

It is when the customer knows you, likes you, trusts you, answers your phone call during a wedding reception, laughs before you talk, says “what’s up bro,” gives you the real budget problem, introduces you to the guy who actually runs the thing, and then somehow asks when your proposal is coming.

This is not normal sales.

This is not networking.

This is not “checking in.”

This is when the relationship has entered the upper atmosphere and procurement can no longer breathe without a special mask.

Section 1: The Ancient Law of Bob

Bob teaches:

People buy from people they know, like, and trust.

This is the whole sandwich.

Some salespeople hear this and say “yes absolutely” then immediately send a seven paragraph email starting with “Hope this finds you well.”

It did not find them well.

It found them deleting you.

The customer does not wake up hoping to be nurtured by a vendor journey.

The customer wakes up with a problem.

A weird problem.

A budget problem.

A schedule problem.

A chemical feed pump problem.

A “my boss asked me why this is not done yet” problem.

A problem standing in the hallway holding coffee and blinking too slow.

Then you appear.

If they do not know you, you are a stranger with attachments.

If they do not like you, you are a calendar invite wearing pants.

If they do not trust you, you are just another guy saying “solution.”

But if they know you, like you, trust you, and have begun saying things like “you should talk to Gary before this gets stupid,” then congratulations.

You may be KLTmaxxing.

Do not celebrate yet.

Gary may still ruin everything.

Section 2: The Four Levels of the Sales Creature

Level 1: Know

They know your name.

Huge.

A raccoon also knows where the trash is.

Do not get proud yet.

At this stage the customer says:

“Who was that guy again?”

or

“The WesTech guy.”

or

“The water guy.”

or

“The guy from LinkedIn who keeps appearing near industrial infrastructure.”

This is fine. This is how life starts. You are a small sales egg in the corporate nest. Do not peck too hard.

Level 2: Like

Now they like you.

Not in a weird way.

Not “come to Thanksgiving.”

Not “be in the family photo.”

More like:

“Actually bring Greg into this. He’ll understand what we’re dealing with.”

This is the L.

The L is very sneaky.

It is not charm. It is not cologne. It is not saying “buddy” to a plant manager you met 14 minutes ago.

The L is when the customer stops treating you like a toll booth and starts treating you like a person who might make the next 30 minutes less dumb.

This is a major sales event.

Fireworks are not provided because finance cut the budget.

Level 3: Trust

Now they trust you.

This is where the relationship gets heavier.

They may say things like:

“Here’s what’s really going on.”
“Don’t put this in writing yet.”
“The RFP doesn’t really describe the problem.”
“Procurement thinks this is about price but operations is scared.”

Very good.

Now you are near the machine room of the deal. Wear eye protection.

Trust means they believe your judgment enough to hand you a piece of the real problem.

Do not drop it.

Do not juggle it.

Do not put it in a “quick recap” email with 11 bullets and a smiley face.

Level 4: KLTmaxxing

This is the forbidden level.

The customer knows you.

The customer likes you.

The customer trusts you.

But now something else is happening.

They see your name on caller ID and instead of saying “not this guy,” they say:

“This is either going to help me or make me laugh.”

Then they answer.

During golf. During dinner. During a wedding reception. During the part of the conference where everyone is pretending the panel is good.

During an airport delay where the only food left is a $19 turkey wrap that has legal issues.

They answer because your interruption has value.

That is KLTmaxxing.

You have become a return on interruption.

That is the phrase.

Write it down.

Actually don’t. It sounds like a software startup.

Trainee Detonation Box 1: Are You Known or Just Nearby?

Select an option and the workbook will judge you gently but not really.
If the answer is “that guy,” you are still in Level 1. Respect the ladder.

Section 3: The L Is Where the Sauce Lives

The L is the funniest part because it sounds soft.

It is not soft.

It is the hinge on the entire barn door.

Without the L the seller is just clanking around outside making “value proposition” noises.

The customer may know you.

The customer may even respect that your company has engineers, brochures, skid photos, and one guy named Steve who knows everything but answers emails like a submarine.

Still not enough.

If they do not like working with you, every conversation costs them energy.

You are now a tax.

No one likes tax.

Except maybe one guy in accounting who has a special calculator and no visible fear.

The L means the customer feels better when you are involved.

Not happier.

Better.

Clearer.

Less alone.

Less trapped in the corporate fog machine.

Maybe slightly entertained.

Maybe they see your email and think:

“Okay this one might not be terrible.”

That is the L.

That is sacred.

Do not ruin it by saying “synergy.”

Section 4: Signs the L Has Arrived

The L may be present when:

  • The customer replies like a human.
  • They stop writing “per my last email.”
  • They use your first name and not “team.”
  • They make a side comment.
  • They tell you travel was a disaster.
  • They tell you the meeting was weird.
  • They admit the schedule is fake.
  • They tell you the budget is “being discussed,” which means everyone is scared but in business casual.
  • They laugh at something you said that was barely a joke.
  • They send you something and say: “Thought you’d appreciate this.”

Huge.

That is not a lead.

That is not a PO.

But it is a small bird landing on your sales shoulder.

Remain calm.

Do not try to sell the bird.

Trainee Detonation Box 2: Diagnose the L Before You Hurt Yourself

Select your L status. The workbook is wearing a tiny judge robe.
Crumbs are not bread. Do not build a house out of crumbs. Ants will come.

Section 5: Wedding Reception Theory

This is the central doctrine.

KLTmaxxing is measured by the Wedding Reception Test.

Question:

Would the customer answer your call during a wedding reception?

Not during the vows.

Do not be insane.

We are sales professionals.

Barely.

But during the reception.

Music playing.

Uncle doing a speech nobody approved.

Chicken or salmon.

Some cousin named Tyler has removed his tie and is about to become a liability.

The customer sees your name.

They look at the phone.

They think:

“This better be useful.”

Then they answer.

That is the test.

Not because you are needy.

Not because you are interrupting.

Not because you are trying to prove dominance over matrimony.

Because you have trained the relationship through repeated usefulness that your call is probably worth the 90 seconds.

That is advanced.

That is rare.

That is very close to illegal in Canada probably.

Trainee Detonation Box 3: Wedding Reception Drill

If your reason is not value or clarity, do not call the wedding. Eat your salmon and reflect.
If you selected “just checking in,” please leave the workbook on the table and walk into the nearest lake. Not deep. Just enough to think.

Section 6: KLTmaxxing Is Not Friendship

Very important.

KLTmaxxing is not friendship.

Friendship is when someone helps you move.

KLTmaxxing is when someone tells you who actually owns the budget before the RFP drops.

Different thing.

Friendship is pizza.

KLTmaxxing is deal intelligence with eye contact.

Do not confuse them.

The customer may like you. They may laugh. They may call you bro. They may greet you with a high five. They may ask about your trip.

They may even say:

“Man we need to get you guys involved earlier next time.”

Beautiful.

But they can still award the project to your competitor because the competitor was cheaper, faster, already approved, better positioned, politically embedded, or golfing with Gary.

Gary again.

Gary is everywhere.

KLTmaxxing does not mean you won.

It means you have unusual access to make winning more likely.

Use the access.

Do not stand there holding it like a decorative squash.

Section 7: The Professional Bro

At KLTmaxxing stage you may be granted one professional bro.

Use carefully.

Correct use:

“Bro, we need to solve the operating model before procurement turns this into a spreadsheet contest.”

Incorrect use:

“Bro can I get that PO today lol.”

Never write “lol” near a purchase order unless you sell inflatable kayaks.

The professional bro is not slang.

It is a cultural instrument.

It says:

  • We are familiar enough to speak plainly.
  • We are still serious enough to move the work.
  • We are not two guys yelling in a Buffalo Wild Wings parking lot.

Unless the customer is actually in a Buffalo Wild Wings parking lot.

In which case adapt.

Sales is situational.

Trainee Detonation Box 4: Professional Bro Authorization Form

Professional bro is a cultural instrument. Do not play it like a kazoo in a boardroom.

Section 8: The Difference Between Being Liked and Being Useful

Some salespeople are liked.

They are pleasant.

They remember names.

They say “good to see you.”

They know where the coffee is.

They can talk sports for nine minutes without causing harm.

Fine.

But being liked is not enough.

A golden retriever is liked.

The golden retriever is not getting sole-source consideration on a complex industrial water project.

Probably.

KLTmaxxing requires usefulness.

You need to bring something.

  • A clearer path.
  • A sharper question.
  • A risk they missed.
  • A way to compress schedule.
  • A person they should meet.
  • A commercial structure.
  • A field reality.
  • A reason not to make the cheap mistake.
  • A sentence that cuts through two weeks of internal fog.

At KLTmaxxing stage the customer likes you because you are useful.

Not instead of useful.

Because.

That is the whole thing hiding inside the clown car.

Section 9: What the Customer Says When You Are KLTmaxxing

They may say:

“Call me.”

This means you are through the first gate.

They may say:

“I’ll tell you what’s really going on.”

This means the gate has opened and there is a smaller scarier gate behind it.

They may say:

“You didn’t hear this from me.”

This means listen carefully and do not become a screenshot.

They may say:

“You should meet our operations lead.”

This means the deal has bones.

They may say:

“We need your team’s view before this gets too far.”

This means you are upstream.

Upstream is good.

Downstream is where vendors go to fight in the mud for 2% margin while procurement watches from a folding chair.

They may say:

“What would you do?”

This is one of the highest signals.

Do not answer like a brochure.

Answer like someone who has seen a jobsite at 6:40 a.m. and understands consequences.

Also maybe eat breakfast first.

Low blood sugar ruins strategy.

The right answer may be Gary. It is often Gary. Still qualify it like a professional.
Some customers do not like hedge-based sales diagnostics. Deploy according to relationship altitude.

Section 10: The Sacred Weapons of KLTmaxxing

At this stage you unlock special weapons.

Not weapons like weapons.

Sales weapons.

Relax HR.

Weapon 1: The Plain Read

“I’ll give you my plain read.”

This is powerful.

It means you are about to say something useful without wrapping it in 600 yards of corporate bubble wrap.

“I’ll give you my plain read. This is being framed as a capex issue but it feels more like an operating risk issue.”

Boom.

A chair moved somewhere.

Weapon 2: The Real Question

“What is actually holding this up?”

Not:

“Where are you in your process?”

That is weak tea.

That is hotel lobby cucumber water.

Ask the real question.

Weapon 3: The Friendly Challenge

“I understand why that option looks cheaper. I’m not sure it is cheaper after startup.”

This is not rude.

This is helpful.

If they like and trust you, they can hear it.

If they do not, they will say “interesting” and bury you in the vendor pit.

Weapon 4: The Meeting Compression Device

“We can keep trading fragments or get the four right people in one room and solve this.”

This is strong.

This creates movement.

This also makes people nervous because now the fake activity has to become real activity.

Good.

Weapon 5: The Close That Does Not Wear Tap Shoes

“If we solve the commercial structure and confirm schedule, is there any reason we should not move forward together?”

Direct.

Clean.

No jazz hands.

No “just wanted to see.”

No soft-shoe routine in the lobby.

Ask like someone who earned the right.

Trainee Detonation Box 6: Choose Your Weapon

Make it direct. Make it human. Make it not weird. Harder than it sounds.

Section 11: How People Ruin KLTmaxxing

Salespeople are amazing because they can spend 14 months building a relationship and then destroy it with one lazy follow-up.

Ruin Method 1: Getting Too Casual

They like me.

I can wing it.

No.

No you cannot.

The customer likes the version of you that was prepared.

Do not replace that person with Airport Thumb Email Guy.

Airport Thumb Email Guy has value in emergencies.

He should not run strategy.

Ruin Method 2: Mistaking Banter for Budget

The customer laughed.

Great.

The CFO did not laugh money into existence.

Find the budget.

Ruin Method 3: Forgetting the Decision Process

You have a champion.

Nice.

Does the champion own the decision?

No?

Then keep mapping.

A champion without authority is still useful.

But do not ask a golden retriever to sign a master service agreement.

Again with the golden retriever.

He is trying his best.

Ruin Method 4: Becoming the Fun Vendor

Danger.

You are funny.

They like you.

They invite you around.

You make meetings better.

Then the serious vendor gets the work.

This is how a man becomes a mascot with mileage reimbursement.

Do not become mascot.

Mascot bad.

Trusted advisor good.

Ruin Method 5: Not Asking

This is the worst.

You earned the relationship.

You got the access.

You saw the opening.

Then you said:

“Happy to help.”

Happy to help is where closes go to be buried in a shallow grave behind the CRM.

Ask for the next step.

Ask for the meeting.

Ask what is missing.

Ask if they want to move forward.

Bob did not train you to become a friendly weather pattern.

Section 12: The KLTmaxxing Emergency Broadcast System

If you believe you are KLTmaxxing, ask yourself:

  • Do they tell me the truth?
  • Do they bring me in early?
  • Do they let me challenge them?
  • Do they tell me who matters?
  • Do they answer when busy?
  • Do they ask my opinion?
  • Do they let me see the problem behind the problem?
  • Do they give me action or just warmth?
  • Do they introduce me to power?
  • Do they still expect excellence?

If yes, congratulations.

You may be in the zone.

Proceed with humility and possibly a clean shirt.

If no, return to normal KLT building.

Do not fake maxx.

Fake maxxing is very dangerous.

It looks like confidence but smells like forecast inflation.

Section 13: KLTmaxxing Role Play

Customer:

“We’re trying to decide if this should be a capital project or outsourced.”

Weak response:

“We can support either way.”

KLTmaxxing response:

“The real question may be whether you want to own the operating risk long term. The capital model is only one part of it.”

Greg response if relationship is dangerously strong:

“That sounds like capex wearing a fake mustache over an operations problem.”

Instructor decides if class is ready.

Most classes are not ready.

Customer:

“Your competitor is cheaper.”

Weak response:

“We can sharpen our pencil.”

Stop sharpening pencils.

There are no pencils left.

KLTmaxxing response:

“Understood. Are they cheaper against the bid tab or cheaper against the risk you actually have to live with?”

Greg response:

“Cheaper at the front door can still rob you in the kitchen.”

This metaphor may require supervision.

Customer:

“We need to think about it.”

Weak response:

“Absolutely. Let me know.”

KLTmaxxing response:

“Makes sense. What part needs more thought?”

Greg response:

“Are we thinking or are we hiding from Gary?”

Only use if Gary is known.

Never invent Gary unless the relationship is elite.

Section 14: The Final Stage — They Name Their Kid After You Probably

The highest known KLTmaxxing legends include:

  • The customer answers during a wedding reception.
  • The customer says “I was just about to call you.”
  • The customer introduces you as “the guy I told you about.”
  • The customer starts using your phrase in internal meetings.
  • The customer tells your competitor “we already have someone helping us think through this.”
  • The customer asks if you can join a meeting before there is a formal project.
  • The customer says “don’t send me a deck, just tell me what you think.”
  • The customer asks your opinion on something adjacent because you have become generally useful.
  • The customer says “bro” in front of procurement and procurement accepts it as part of the record.
  • The customer names their child after you.

This last one is rare.

Usually a middle name.

Do not request it.

Let the market decide.

Section 15: KLTmaxxing Is Still Value Selling

This appendix is insane.

But the concept is not.

KLTmaxxing still lives inside the Value Selling framework.

It is not a replacement for process.

It is what happens when the process has worked so well that the relationship becomes a strategic asset.

You still need to understand:

  • Need.
  • Value.
  • Timing.
  • Decision process.
  • Authority.
  • Risk.
  • Alternatives.
  • Differentiation.
  • The cost of doing nothing.
  • How much.
  • How soon.
  • How sure.

You still need to prepare.

You still need to follow through.

You still need to close.

KLTmaxxing is not skipping the process.

KLTmaxxing is what happens when the relationship gives you permission to run the process at a higher altitude with fewer fake conversations and more truth.

That is the serious part.

Now back to nonsense.
